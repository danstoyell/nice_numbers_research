# Nice numbers: quick research note

Researched September 23, 2026. Scope: the square–cube pandigital problem associated with Conflux and Manifold. This is a brief public-source survey, not an exhaustive literature review.

**The concept.** A positive integer `n` is nice in integer base `b ≥ 2` when the ordinary base-`b` representations of `n²` and `n³`, taken together, contain each digit `0…b−1` exactly once. No leading-zero padding. The canonical example is `69² = 4761`, `69³ = 328509`. The project still identifies `(n, b) = (69, 10)` as the only known example. [Nice Numbers project](https://nicenumbers.net/)

**Why those keywords matter.** Jacob Cohen writes as **Chromatic Conflux** and organized this exploration through his blog and **Manifold Markets**, the prediction-market platform. His December 17, 2022 post popularized searching across bases. The original market was cancelled after someone pointed out that its wording admitted `−69`; this explains the explicit positive-integer restriction. [Original blog post](https://beautifulthorns.wixsite.com/home/post/is-69-unique), [original market and clarification](https://manifold.markets/Conflux/will-i-learn-of-a-nice-number-besid)

**The mathematical picture.** Several exact constraints make searching easier:

- **Digit count:** `floor(2 log_b n) + floor(3 log_b n) + 2 = b`. This confines candidates to a finite interval for each base and rules out every base `b ≡ 1 (mod 5)`. In decimal, only `47 ≤ n ≤ 99` qualify by length. [Original analysis](https://beautifulthorns.wixsite.com/home/post/is-69-unique)
- **Last digits:** reject residues where `n² ≡ n³ (mod b)`, because those powers would share their final digit.
- **Digit sum:** necessarily `n² + n³ ≡ b(b−1)/2 (mod b−1)`. Filtering residue classes by this condition eliminates substantial work. It also rules out `b ≡ 3 (mod 4)`: the left side is even, while the required residue is odd modulo an even modulus. These are necessary conditions, not sufficient tests. [Conflux’s January 30, 2023 update](https://beautifulthorns.wixsite.com/home/post/progress-update-on-the-search-for-nice-numbers)

**Why people expect more.** A crude random-digit model assigns a candidate probability `b! / b^b`; multiplying by the candidate count gives an expected number of solutions. Though individual candidates become less likely to work, the search intervals grow fast enough that the model predicts abundance in sufficiently large admissible bases, with the interesting transition roughly around bases 120–130. This is a heuristic, not a proof of existence or infinitude; an expected count is not itself a probability. [Original heuristic](https://beautifulthorns.wixsite.com/home/post/is-69-unique)

The refined thinking accounts for modular dependencies. Experiments with the easier analogue where `n` and `n²` jointly cover all digits (“quasi-nice” numbers) support residue-adjusted estimates. Joshua Brower also reports that prime bases’ apparent density advantage largely disappears after conditioning on distinct final digits of the square and cube. These observations inform search strategy without proving the model. [Conflux’s update](https://beautifulthorns.wixsite.com/home/post/progress-update-on-the-search-for-nice-numbers), [Prime Bases and Nice Numbers, January 8, 2023](https://manifold.markets/post/prime-bases-and-nice-numbers)

**The project hub: nicenumbers.net.** Wasabipesto’s site coordinates distributed searching and displays near misses, digit-diversity statistics, completion, search rates, and contributor rankings. Its browser client lets volunteers search assigned ranges; the linked Rust implementation supports native clients. “Niceness” measures distinct digits divided by the base for candidates of the correct length. The highlighted `330,169,542,960,890` in base 45 has 44 distinct digits across its square and cube: approximately 97.8% nice. I independently checked that count with integer arithmetic. [Project dashboard](https://nicenumbers.net/), [near-miss data](https://data.nicenumbers.net/cache_notable_numbers?select=number,niceness,base,num_uniques&order=off_by.desc)

**Latest progress I could substantiate.** The older write-up’s base-48 milestone has been surpassed in the dashboard data. On September 23, 2026, every listed chunk in bases 49, 50, 52, 53, and 54 was marked searched at least in nice-only mode. Base 57 had 12 of 100 chunks searched, representing approximately 12.002% of its candidate interval. The intervening bases 51, 55, and 56 are mathematically excluded by the constraints above. The listed intervals for these searched bases are contiguous. This is the server’s reported coverage, not an independent rerun of the search. [Live completion data](https://data.nicenumbers.net/chunks?select=id,base_id,range_start,range_end,range_size,minimum_cl&base_id=gte.35&order=id.asc), [older search report](https://wasabipesto.com/nice/)

The retrieved near-miss table contained 896 records and only one with niceness 1: 69 in base 10. I found no verified announcement of a second example or an existence proof. These figures are a dated snapshot and will change as work continues. [Near-miss data](https://data.nicenumbers.net/cache_notable_numbers?select=number,niceness,base,num_uniques&order=off_by.desc)

Development continued into 2026: the repository history records experimental CUDA support on January 16 and multi-digit suffix filtering with a Chinese Remainder Theorem stride table on February 8. The retrieved README now describes CUDA and CubeCL GPU backends, alongside distributed CPU searching and separate detailed-statistics versus nice-only modes. These are concrete computational advances; they do not establish a new mathematical solution. [Commit history](https://github.com/wasabipesto/nice/commits/main/), [search implementation and README](https://github.com/wasabipesto/nice)

My working assessment: further solutions are plausible under the refined heuristics, but locating one remains the central challenge. Any future search should combine exact interval bounds, modular filters, early duplicate detection, and independent verification of candidate discoveries.
